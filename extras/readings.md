## Foundations & Classical Reconstructions (Pre-Qin & Han)

Landmark analytical and historical works that transformed modern understandings of ancient Chinese disputation, cosmological emergence, and semantic theory.

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Disputers of the Tao: Philosophical Argument in Ancient China* | A. C. Graham | Landmark Monograph | Graham's magnum opus provides an unrivaled analytical reconstruction of classical Chinese thought, tracking the development of reason, dialectics, and the breakdown of traditional social order. |
| *The World of Thought in Ancient China* | Benjamin I. Schwartz | Landmark Monograph | A monumental comparative history exploring the deep tension between immanent cosmological order and transcendent moral authority in early Chinese thought. |
| *Thinking Through Confucius* | David L. Hall & Roger T. Ames | Philosophical Monograph | Pioneered the non-transcendental, process-oriented, and aesthetic interpretive model of classical Confucianism, emphasizing contextual personhood and ritual harmony. |
| *A Daoist Theory of Chinese Thought: A Philosophical Interpretation* | Chad Hansen | Philosophical Monograph | A groundbreaking, provocative semantic critique arguing that classical Chinese philosophy centers on pragmatic action-guiding discourse (*dao*) rather than Western representational truth. |
| *To Become a God: Cosmology, Sacrifice, and Self-Divinization in Early China* | Michael Puett | Historical Monograph | Challenges the common assumption of natural organic harmony in early China, demonstrating that cosmology and sacrifice were fierce battlegrounds over human and divine agency. |

## Medieval Ontology, Xuanxue, & Buddhist Dialectics (Wei-Jin to Tang)

Field-defining philosophical investigations into post-Han ontology, Chinese Madhyamaka, Tiantai dialectics, Huayan mereology, and Chan deconstruction.

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *The Penumbra Unbound: The Neo-Taoist Philosophy of Guo Xiang* | Brook Ziporyn | Landmark Monograph | An extraordinary, rigorous analysis of Guo Xiang's *Zhuangzi* commentary, uncovering a radical ontology of immanent self-generation (*ziran*) and solitary emergence (*duhua*). |
| *Being and Ambiguity: Philosophical Experiments with Tiantai Buddhism* | Brook Ziporyn | Philosophical Monograph | A sophisticated reconstruction of Zhiyi's Threefold Truth, exploring the paradoxical Tiantai doctrine that evil and delusion are inherently entailed within absolute reality. |
| *Hua-yen Buddhism: The Jewel Net of Indra* | Francis H. Cook | Philosophical Monograph | The definitive English-language philosophical exposition of Huayan mereology, elucidating the universal interpenetration and mutual identity of all concrete phenomena (*shishi wu'ai*). |
| *Buddhist Phenomenology: A Philosophical Investigation of Yogacara Buddhism and the Ch'eng Wei-shih Lun* | Dan Lusthaus | Landmark Monograph | A bridge between Indian-Chinese Yogacara epistemology and Western phenomenological traditions (Husserl and Merleau-Ponty), reframing Consciousness-Only as an epistemic critique rather than idealism. |
| *Seeing through Zen: Encounter, Transformation, and Genealogy in Chinese Chan Buddhism* | John R. McRae | Historical/Critical Monograph | Demystifies Chan romanticism by analyzing the performative and pedagogical logic of encounter dialogues (*gong'an*) and lineage construction. |

## Neo-Confucian Metaphysics & Moral Psychology (Song to Qing)

Foundational works analyzing the theoretical synthesis of Song, Yuan, Ming, and early Qing thinkers.

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Sagehood: The Contemporary Significance of Neo-Confucian Philosophy* | Stephen C. Angle | Philosophical Monograph | Systematically reconstructs Neo-Confucian moral epistemology and the metaphysics of principle (*li*), establishing their vital relevance to contemporary virtue ethics and value theory. |
| *Confucian Moral Self-Cultivation* | Philip J. Ivanhoe | Monograph / Critical Survey | Traces the evolution of self-cultivation paradigms from Confucius to Wang Yangming, comparing developmental sprouting models against discovery and habituation models. |
| *Waiting for the Dawn: A Plan for the Prince (Mingyi Daifanglu)* | Huang Zongxi (trans. Wm. Theodore de Bary) | Primary Text & Critical Monograph | The seminal late-Ming constitutional treatise offering a radical critique of imperial autocracy and articulating institutional checks on sovereign power. |
| *The Religious Dimension of Confucianism* | Rodney L. Taylor | Monograph | Illuminates the profound ultimate concern, spiritual praxis, and contemplative depth embedded within Neo-Confucian self-transformation. |

## Seminal Essays & Interpretive Breakthroughs

Influential short monographs, essay collections, and breakthrough papers that altered modern scholarly discourse.

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Confucius: The Secular as Sacred* | Herbert Fingarette | Landmark Book / Extended Essay | A classic paradigm-shifting text that rescued Confucian ritual (*li*) from charges of empty conventionalism, showing how ritual creates sacred human community without Cartesian dualism. |
| *Mencius and Early Chinese Thought* | Kwong-loi Shun | Landmark Monograph | The most linguistically and conceptually thorough analytical study of Mencius's moral psychology, human nature (*xing*), and ethical sprouting theory. |
| *Essays on Skepticism, Relativism, and Ethics in the Zhuangzi* | Paul Kjellberg & Philip J. Ivanhoe (eds.) | Seminal Essay Collection | A defining anthology gathering diverse analytical perspectives on radical semantic skepticism, perspectival relativism, and therapeutic mysticism in the *Zhuangzi*. |
| *Ritual and Deference: Extending Chinese Philosophy in a Comparative Context* | David L. Hall & Roger T. Ames | Essay Collection | Articulates the philosophical significance of deference, aesthetic order, and relational sociality in contrast to Western individualistic rights models. |

## Modern Reconstructions, Role Ethics, & Contemporary Political Theory

Major twentieth-century syntheses and twenty-first-century applied political and ethical frameworks.

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Nineteen Lectures on Chinese Philosophy and World Philosophy* | Mou Zongsan (trans. Jason Cliver & Bryan W. Van Norden) | Landmark Lecture Monograph | The intellectual summit of Modern New Confucianism, synthesizing Kantian moral philosophy with Tiantai, Huayan, and Xiong Shili's metaphysics of moral autocracy. |
| *The Examination of the Self in Role Ethics: A New Paradigm for Comparative Ethics* | Henry Rosemont Jr. & Roger T. Ames | Philosophical Monograph | Formulates Confucian "role ethics" (*lunli*) as an independent normative framework that resists reduction to Western deontology, utilitarianism, or Aristotelian virtue ethics. |
| *Confucian Perfectionism: A Deliberative Stance* | Joseph Chan | Political Theory Monograph | A landmark contribution to contemporary political theory, constructing a public-reason Confucian framework that synthesizes classical perfectionism with human rights and democratic participation. |
| *The China Model: Political Meritocracy and the Limits of Democracy* | Daniel A. Bell | Political Philosophy Monograph | An influential, widely debated defense of political meritocracy, examining how Confucian administrative selection offers structural alternatives to electoral democracy. |
